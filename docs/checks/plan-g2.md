# Plan G2: what ran, and what to check by hand

The run was on the `plan-g2` branch, starting from commit 26a16d6, on a 16-core Linux laptop with an Intel Core Ultra 7 255H, its integrated Intel graphics and a discrete NVIDIA card, with Rust 1.98.0, in a release build, on 2026-11-19. It found one thing to fix, with a test that failed before its fix: a view set or added by hand was written with every figure a drag's sums leave, as *320.99945 26.00008 15.515446*. A second change lets a window write how many pictures of a turning model it draws and releases each second, which is how the turning below was measured, and which the hand checks use. Both are under *What it found*. The branch hasn't been pushed or merged yet. Nothing here has run on Windows.

Every model in this run and in the tests was made by the model crate's own test makers, `slides_model::make_test_glb` and `make_test_ply`: in the window, a flat square grid of 707 by 707 squares, 999,698 triangles, coloured from a 2,048-pixel JPEG texture in four quarters (22 MB, called *the terrain* below, though it's flat); a box a unit a side with three materials (2 KB); and a cloud of five million points over a sphere, each coloured by where it lies (75 MB). None of the owner's own models was used. **Made models are far simpler to draw than scanned or modelled ones**: one texture, no overlapping parts, nothing hidden, so every drawing time below flatters. The spikes' record, `docs/checks/live-content-spikes.md`, section 2, has the times for real ones.

## Tests on Linux

At the run's last commit, `scripts/cargo-safe.sh test --workspace` passed: 2,486 tests, 0 failed, 35 ignored, no warnings. The four new ignored ones are the release timing tests below. `scripts/cargo-safe.sh clippy --workspace --all-targets` gave no warnings.

The random editing session, `crates/app/tests/typing_random.rs`, has its three parts as before: typing, arranging, and the window. The window's part now has three more kinds of step among its 70, drawn on the software adapter where there is one: a made box put on the slide, from the Model tool's dialog or dropped at a random place; a model on the slide selected and turned or done, by its button or Space, and while it's turned dragged round or zoomed with the wheel; and a model turned, dragged round and its view written with *Set view*, then done or not. The show's step now and then drags in one of the show's windows, which turns a model there. After every step, besides the checks it had, a model is turned in the editor only while it alone is selected; at the end none is turned and none is held on the graphics card. A count across the runs makes sure they did it: in the default run of 20, the steps put 34 models on slides, turned one 19 times and set 10 views. The session passed, the window part in 8 minutes 27 seconds. It found nothing to fix.

The release timing tests, `scripts/cargo-safe.sh test --release -p stagepack-studio --test edit_speed -- --ignored`, ran in two conditions. While the laptop was on its charger, the four new ones ran twice on the best adapter and once on each of the others; the table below has those. Later, on its battery, the whole suite ran three times: see after the table. The four new ones are on a talk of ten slides, each a heading, words, a list and a model of 800 by 450, five of them a textured grid of 20,000 triangles and five the box, none turned; and on the terrain turning on its own in the show, filling the slide. With `ADAPTER=discrete`, `integrated` or `software` they draw on that kind of adapter alone; without, on the best there is, which here is the discrete card. They ran on each:

| What | Discrete card | Integrated card | Software (lavapipe) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A keystroke beside a model, on a talk of ten models, the slide laid out and drawn again: median (slowest) | 3.2 ms (9.2 ms) | 3.3 ms (12.7 ms) | 3.2 ms (11.0 ms) |
| A frame of a drag of a model on that talk: median (slowest) | 0.011 to 0.013 ms (0.06 ms) | 0.025 ms (0.09 ms) | 0.011 ms (0.05 ms) |
| Opening that talk as the window does, every slide drawn small and the first drawn, against the same talk without its models | 12.1 to 13.8 ms against 10.8 to 12.0 ms | 13.7 ms against 13.3 ms | 11.4 ms against 11.4 ms |
| Its ten stills drawn after, one after another, off the window's thread | 44 to 45 ms | 126 ms | 131 ms |
| The terrain turning for 300 pictures: how long it took | 5.6 to 6.6 s | 35.5 s | 69.6 s |
| The same: each frame that showed a new picture, on the window's thread: median (99th percentile) | 0.29 ms (4.5 ms) | 1.9 ms (8.1 ms) | 0.19 ms (0.65 ms) |
| The same: every frame the window drew meanwhile: median (99th percentile) | 0.20 ms (0.63 ms) | 0.27 ms (0.84 ms) | 0.22 ms (1.6 ms) |

The budgets are 8 ms for a keystroke and 6 ms for a frame of a drag, at the median, and opening may take half a second more than without models: all hold. Typing beside a model or dragging it draws nothing, and opening the talk draws nothing either: the tests count what's asked of the drawer. The window draws nothing to a screen in these tests, so painting the picture isn't in their numbers; the window's thread only takes in a picture the drawer has drawn and a background task has made the window's image, and asks for the next. **The terrain is drawn about 50 times a second on the discrete card, 8 on the integrated card and 4 in software**: drawing a million triangles and bringing the picture back is the drawer's own thread's work, which the window never waits for, but it is how often a turning model changes on the screen. The integrated card's frames that take in a picture are slower on the window's thread, 1.9 ms at the median, within the budget; why isn't known: its read-back is copied on several threads, which share the processor with the window, which may be it. **On its battery the laptop ran about twice as slowly**, its cores at 1.6 to 1.8 GHz, a virus scanner and the desktop's file indexer busy: opening the talk of ten slides without models took 27 ms against 11 ms on the charger. In the three runs of the whole suite then, a keystroke beside a model took 6.1 to 7.4 ms at the median (slowest 11.5 to 16.8 ms), a drag frame of one 0.027 ms, opening the talk 31 ms against 27 ms without models, its stills 64 to 78 ms, and the terrain's frames that took in a picture 1.0 to 2.2 ms at the median and 7.6 to 8.4 ms at the 99th percentile on the discrete card: all within their budgets. **One test from before failed in all three, and alone after**: a keystroke in a table cell on the 500-slide talk, `a_keystroke_in_a_table_cell_is_quick`, took 9.7 to 12.2 ms at the median against its 8 ms. It types in a table and nothing of this plan's is in it; G1 had it within budget on the charger, with its slowest keystroke at 19.8 ms. It should be run again on the charger before the branch is merged. The other timing tests from before ran within their budgets.

## The scratch display, and the owner's data

As in plan G1: a nested X display, `Xephyr :87` at 1,600 by 900 with no window manager; every launch through one script that refuses to start without that display and a scratch message bus, with `DISPLAY=:87`, `WAYLAND_DISPLAY` and `STUDIO_BUNDLE` taken away, `HOME` set to a scratch home in the session's scratch folder, `XDG_DATA_HOME`, `XDG_CONFIG_HOME`, `XDG_CACHE_HOME`, `XDG_STATE_HOME` and `XDG_DOCUMENTS_DIR` set to folders in it, and a `Documents` folder there. The message bus's socket was in a short scratch folder of its own, as a socket's path can't be as long as the scratch folder's. The running app's environment was read back from `/proc` to be sure. The system's file dialogs were answered by D5's stand-in for the desktop portal on that bus, which writes down what each Open and Save asked and answers with a path. Keys, clicks, drags and the wheel went in through the X test extension, and files were dropped on the window with D3's small program speaking the drag-and-drop protocol. Screenshots were kept in the scratch folder, not committed; every one was looked at.

The real data folders, `~/.local/share/stagepack-studio` and `~/.cache/stagepack-studio`, were listed with every file's time and size before the first launch and after the last: the two lists, of 25,134 entries each, are the same. The modification times of the two folders and of the real `~/Documents` are the same before and after, and so is the list of what's in `~/Documents`; only the home folder's own time, which other programs on the desktop change, moved. The made models, the saved pack, the exports and the scratch home, about 200 MB, were deleted after it.

The nested display draws in software and shows only so many pictures a second, whatever the app asks, as the spikes and G1 found: here 7 to 9 a second in a window of 1,280 by 800, and about 15 in the rehearsal's smaller audience window. So how smooth a turning model looks can't be judged here: that is the first hand check. What was measured instead, with the counts the app now writes when asked (below): pictures of models drawn and shown, and released, each second; processor use and memory, read from `/proc` each second. The model drawer itself doesn't draw on the nested display: it makes its own device on the computer's best adapter, here the discrete card, whatever display the window is on.

## Real-window check on Linux

### What it did

In three sessions of the app, with a crash before the third, started with `STUDIO_VIDEO_COUNTS=1`:

- **A presentation, and the terrain:** *Light* on the start screen gave *Untitled presentation*; a title, *Models*, was typed. *Model* on the Insert tab, in its group *3D models*, asked the dialog for a file (*Insert*, several allowed), and the stand-in answered the terrain. It was on the slide within a second, in the middle at 960 by 540, framed whole, selected, with *Turn* at its middle and *Model selected* on the status line; the copy was too quick to catch its bar. Its form says *Model*, its name *terrain.glb*, *999,698 triangles*, *Turns on its own* unticked, *Views*: *No views* with *Add this view* and *Clear views*, and its place. The list of slides shows its still. Resident memory went from 306 MB to 353 MB, at most 386 MB.
- **Turned in the editor:** *Turn* became *Set view* and *Done*, and the live picture came about 2.4 seconds after, the model being read from its file and put on the card then. A drag turned it round as far as the pointer went, and the wheel took it further away and nearer; nothing was written meanwhile. While dragged, 9 pictures were drawn and shown in 2.8 seconds and 9 released, the nested display's pace. *Set view* wrote the view shown as the model's starting view and drew its still again there, in the editor and the list of slides. Turned again, dragged to two other places and *Add this view* pressed at each, the form said *2 views*. *Done* put the still back.
- **The box, dropped:** a new slide with Ctrl+M, and the box dropped on it: on the slide in the middle, *box-of-materials.glb*, *12 triangles*, and *Turns on its own* ticked in its form.
- **The cloud:** a third slide, and the cloud from the Model tool: on the slide within a second, *cloud.ply*, *5 million points*, its still a sphere of colours, a plain panel for the moment before. Reading and framing it took resident memory from 407 MB to 491 MB at most, and back to 433 MB. Turned, dragged and stopped with Space.
- **A text file named `.glb`:** from the Model tool, the status line said *This file isn't a 3D model.* and nothing was put on the slide.
- **The show:** F5 presented from the first slide, the terrain as its still. Right flew it to its first view and a click to its second, each a step of the slide, and a drag on it turned it without moving the show on; the wheel zoomed it. Right went to the box, turning on its own: 7 pictures drawn and shown a second, every one released, 0.96 of a core, 494 MB. Right to the cloud, which a drag turned; Left back to the box, turning from its view again. Escape ended the show; the window, idle, used 0.01 of a core, 481 MB, 73 threads, as many as during the show.
- **Five minutes turning:** the cloud given *Turns on its own*, and the rehearsal (Shift+F5, which starts at the first slide with the presenter's window beside the audience's) stepped on to it. It turned on its own for 300 seconds: **memory 517 MB, flat, every second**; 0.86 of a core on average, the last 270 seconds 0.85; 15.5 pictures drawn and shown a second and 15.5 released, 4,727 of each, so every picture shown was let go of; 73 threads throughout. The presenter's window showed the cloud's still. Before that, the rehearsal sat five minutes on the first slide, nothing turning: 0.08 of a core and 498 MB, flat.
- **Saved as a pack, closed, opened, turned:** Ctrl+S asked *Keep the talk as a folder of files, or pack it into one file to send.*; *Packed talk (.sptx)* asked the dialog for *Untitled presentation.sptx*, the stand-in answered `Models.sptx`, and the status line said *Saved Models.sptx.*, the title *Models · 3 / 3*. Resident memory while saving stayed at 516 to 518 MB. The pack holds the three models stored, not compressed, as the plan has it, and its text has each `<Model>` with its view, the terrain's two views and the others' `turn="auto"`. The window closed without asking, and the app ended. Started again, *Open a talk file…* opened `Models.sptx` with its three stills; the terrain was turned from the pack, its view set, and Ctrl+S saved in place: *Saved Models.sptx.*
- **A crash, and the talk brought back:** Ctrl+N, *Dark*, a title, and the terrain dropped on it; five seconds later the app was ended with `kill -9`. Its working copy held the terrain. Started again, the start screen listed it under *Not saved last time*, *Untitled presentation*, *Crash*, *1 slide*; *Open* showed it with its model, which turned.
- **A slide copied to another talk:** with `Models.sptx` open in a second window, its cloud's slide was copied from the list of slides and pasted into the brought-back talk. The slide came with its cloud, the 75 MB file in that talk's working copy; a model under 256 MB is copied without asking, as a video is.
- **Exported:** *PDF…* and *PowerPoint…* from the Export menu of `Models.sptx`, answered with files in a scratch folder: each said *Exported Models.pdf. 3 models were exported as pictures.* (and *.pptx*). The PDF has 3 pages of 960 by 540 points, each model its still at its starting view. The PowerPoint file, turned into a PDF by LibreOffice with a scratch profile, shows the same, the three stills as pictures in it.
- **Idle, and closing:** with two windows open and nothing turning, the app used 0.02 of a core and 425 MB. The pack's window closed without asking; the brought-back one asked *Save before closing?*, *Don't save* closed it, the app ended, and its working copy was gone.

### What it found

- **A view set or added by hand was written with every figure a drag's sums leave**, as `view="320.99945 26.00008 15.515446 0 0.500002 0"`, where a model put on a slide is written with its angles to a thousandth of a degree and its distance to four figures. The plan has a view written so a person can read and change it. *Set view* and *Add this view* now write a view as a model put on a slide is: the same view set again in the window gave `view="302.999 29 3.194 0 0 0"`. Test: `a_view_turned_to_by_hand_is_written_with_a_few_figures` in `crates/app/tests/ui_model.rs`.
- **Nothing counted the pictures of a turning model outside a test**, so how a model turns couldn't be measured in the window. With `STUDIO_VIDEO_COUNTS` set, as for videos, the window now writes, each second a model turns, a line such as *model pictures in 1.02 s: 7 drawn and shown, 7 released* to the standard error. Test: `model_counts_are_written_once_a_second_at_most` in `crates/app/src/slide_canvas.rs`.

### What looks wrong and wasn't changed

- **A model dropped on the slide goes in its middle**, framed at 960 by 540, not where it was dropped, as a video does.
- **The first live picture took about 2.4 seconds after *Turn* for the terrain and 3.2 for the cloud**, as each is read and put on the card each time it's turned; meanwhile the still shows, unchanged, and nothing says it's coming.
- **A flat model seen from behind shows nothing**: the made grid has one side, as a glTF surface has unless it says otherwise, so a drag far enough round left an empty box. That's the model, not the app, but a person may take it for a fault.
- **The status line kept *This file isn't a 3D model.*** after the show and in the other window until something else was said.
- **The Model tool's Open dialog offers no list of kinds of file** (it asks for any file, as the Picture and Video tools do), so a person isn't steered to `.glb` and `.ply`. The real dialog's look is a hand check.
- **The Save dialog for a new talk suggested the scratch home, not its Documents folder**: the scratch home had no `user-dirs.dirs` naming one, which is where the app looks. On a desktop that has the file it suggests Documents, as D5 found.

## Not exercised

- **How smooth any model turns or flies**, on any real display. The nested display shows 7 to 15 pictures a second.
- **Anything at all on Windows**, including the drawer's DirectX 12 device and its software adapter (WARP).
- **The owner's own models**, scanned or modelled, with many parts, large textures and points in the tens of millions, and how long they take to read and draw in the app.
- **The timing on other computers' cards**: the figures above are this laptop's discrete NVIDIA card, its integrated Intel graphics and Mesa's software renderer (lavapipe), each chosen with `ADAPTER`; the window run drew on the discrete card only.
- **The system's real file dialogs**, for *Model*, Save As, Open and Export.
- **A second screen**, and the show's presenter window with a model (covered by a headless test: it shows the still).
- **A computer with no adapter at all**, or a card lost while drawing: headless tests only, with the drawer's test hooks.
- **Cancel** pressed on a model's copy: the copies here were too quick to catch; covered by headless tests.
- **Desktop PowerPoint**: the PowerPoint file was drawn by LibreOffice.
- **A drop after a key press, in the headless tests**: in a test window a drop that comes after any key press doesn't reach the slide, which no one has explained; the tests drop first. Drops in the real window, after key presses, arrived.

## Hand check on a real Linux display

Under an X11 session and a Wayland session, from a release build started with `STUDIO_VIDEO_COUNTS=1` in a terminal, so that it writes its counts. Do it three times: once as it is, which draws on the best card there is; once with the discrete card switched off or absent (the card disabled in the firmware, or on a computer without one), so that it draws on the integrated card — on a laptop with both, `DRI_PRIME=0` and `__NV_PRIME_RENDER_OFFLOAD=0` may not stop the drawer choosing the discrete card, as it asks for the best adapter there is: that hasn't been checked, so see in its counts which card drew, and know that the integrated card's figures above come from the timing tests run with `ADAPTER=integrated`, not from a window; and once with Mesa's software renderer removed, or with no Vulkan driver but the card's own, to see that it doesn't fall back to something it can't use.

1. Make or download a binary glTF (`.glb`) of a few hundred thousand triangles with a texture, and a point cloud (`.ply`) of a few million points. Put each on a slide with *Model*, press *Turn*, and drag it round for half a minute. Say whether it turns smoothly, and what the counts say: about as many pictures drawn a second as the screen shows is smooth.
2. The same in the show, F5, with *Turns on its own* ticked and the model filling the slide; and the clicks through two views added with *Add this view*: say whether each flight over a second looks smooth.
3. Three of your own models, the largest among them: put each on a slide, turn it in the editor and in the show, and say whether any stutters, what the counts say, and whether any looks wrong (dark, inside out, missing parts).
4. Watch a model turning on its own for a minute: say whether any picture tears or flickers, and whether the window's own drawing (the slide list, the notes) stays quick while it turns.
5. Leave a model turning on its own in the show for ten minutes, and say what the system monitor shows of the app's memory: it should stay flat.
6. *Model* on the Insert tab: say whether the system's Open dialog shows, and whether you can find `.glb` and `.ply` files in it easily.
7. Present a talk with a turning model on a second screen, with Shift+F5's presenter window on the first: the model should turn on the audience's screen and show its still on the presenter's.
8. Say how long the first picture takes after *Turn* the first time the app draws a model, and after that.

## Windows check, by hand

The branch hasn't been pushed or merged yet. Once it's in `main`, this check starts from a clone of `main`, on a Windows PC as in `docs/checks/plan-d5.md`.

1. `cargo test --workspace`. The model tests that draw use the software adapter where there is one (WARP, on Windows) and skip, saying so, where there's none: say how many skipped.
2. `cargo test --release -p stagepack-studio --test edit_speed -- --ignored`, and again with `set ADAPTER=software`: send back the lines they print.
3. `cargo build --release -p stagepack-studio`, and start it from a terminal after `set STUDIO_VIDEO_COUNTS=1`. The window draws with DirectX; the model is drawn by wgpu with a DirectX 12 device of its own.
4. Steps 1 to 5 and 7 of the Linux check above.
5. *Model* on the Insert tab: say whether the Open dialog shows `.glb` and `.ply` files, and put a model on a slide by dragging it from Explorer.
6. Save the talk packed, close it, and open it again while a model turns in another window of the same talk: say whether the save says anything about the file being in use.
7. Export it to PowerPoint and open it in desktop PowerPoint: each model should be its still picture.
8. Quit the app while a model turns: it should end within a few seconds, with no error from the graphics driver.
9. D5's and G1's Windows checks, where they're still open.

## Known after the run

- **The drawing times were taken on one laptop's cards**: its discrete NVIDIA card, its integrated Intel graphics, and lavapipe. Another computer's card, and Windows's, may be slower or faster.
- **A model is drawn on the computer's best card**, which on a laptop may wake the discrete card and use its battery.
- **Not in this plan:** Gaussian splats; map tiles; models made of several files; animation inside a model; lights and materials beyond a model's own colours and one texture lit simply; 3D shapes drawn by a component's code; models in PowerPoint as 3D objects.
